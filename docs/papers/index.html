News Selection and Household Inflation Expectations

Ryan Chahrour, Adam Hale Shapiro, and Dan Wilson

Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco Working Paper

Revised December 2025

Conditionally accepted, AEJ: Macroeconomics

PDF

Abstract

We examine how media reporting about inflation impacts household inflation expectations. In a model where households learn about inflation from news coverage, households account for news selection—the media’s systematic selection of topics— when forming their expectations. Because media are more likely to report on inflation when it is high, the model implies an asymmetric response to news: high-inflation news changes expectations more than low-inflation news. We test this implication using household panel data, and find that exposure to higher-prices news increases inflation expectations by 0.4 percentage point, while exposure to lower-prices news has no significant effect.
