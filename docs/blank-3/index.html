Measuring Inflation Shock Momentum

Kevin J. Lansing and Adam Hale Shapiro

FRBSF Working Paper

Revised November 2026

Download .bib

Abstract

We develop a non-parametric filter that identifies sustained directional runs in shocks to monthly inflation—a concept we define as “inflation shock momentum.” By assessing the shocks to over 100 disaggregated Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) inflation categories, we isolate the share of categories experiencing positive or negative inflation shock momentum in a given month. We define the “Inflation Shock Momentum Index” (ISMI) as the net positive momentum share of expenditure-weighted categories (positive minus negative) in a given month. We examine the information in ISMI for aggregate PCE inflation at horizons of 1 to 3 years, controlling for other inflation predictors. Adding ISMI improves in-sample fit and generally lowers out-of-sample forecast errors in OLS comparisons with otherwise identical models, particularly at longer horizons, with smaller incremental gains under ridge regularization.
